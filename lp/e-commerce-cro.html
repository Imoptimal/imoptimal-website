Conversion rate optimization for small business websites

If you downloaded my 'Website Foundations' checklist, you already fixed all of the technical aspects of your small business website (speed, accessibility, security and privacy). We've also analyzed all of the individual basic parts of your website, and optimized them for conversion.

If not, grab it now, and first go through the basics. Just follow the short weekly tutorials you'll get in your email inbox.

But if you're running an e-commerce website, that includes any type of online sales (services, digital products, online courses, memberships, etc.) - you should also optimize parts of your website crucial for the purchasing process.

1. Checkout

It's hard enough convincing somebody to buy your product or service. Don't make it difficult for them to go through the checkout process. Here's how to make it as easy as possible:

  • Simplify it - make it a one-page checkout, and minimize the number of required fields. Only ask for essential information (email, shipping address and payment info).
  • Offer guest checkout - because the forced account creation increases the percentage of abandoned carts. At least offer the option for people to log in with their existing social accounts, it speeds the process significantly.
  • Provide multiple payment options - not everyone uses a single payment provider, so you need to cater for that. Besides the card payments, at least offer the digital wallets option (like PayPal).
  • Be transparent - display all of the costs upfront, including all of the fees (shipping and taxes). If possible, offer free shipping. Also, include the link to your return/refund policy, to reassure your customers.
  • Enable cart saving - let users return to their cart through their account or email (if it's guest checkout). Send reminders with incentives (like a discount).
  • Improve error handling - highlight errors clearly (invalid email format, or card number mistake), and let users know what they need to do to complete the checkout. Make sure the form doesn't reset while users try to fix their input errors.

2. Pricing section/page

If someone has already decided they want to work with you, don't make it complicated for them to choose the right offer because of pricing.

Here's what to do if you're a small business that's service oriented (or sells digital products):

  • Simplify pricing structure - limit the number of price options. Too many choices overwhelm and confuse people. Use clear and descriptive name for each option, and highlight the most popular one.
  • Use anchor pricing - you can influence people's decision by placing the highest priced option next to the one you want them to choose. This makes it seem more affordable. You can also offer a discount if they choose the annual billing, to encourage them to stay longer.
  • Highlight the value - help people justify the cost by focusing on the benefits they get. Use bullet points to show the added value for each price option.
  • Make pricing transparent - display all costs upfront (like setup fees, taxes, or add-ons). Avoid "Contact Us' for pricing like you have something to hide.
  • Offer a free trial or a freemium option - it lowers the barrier to entry. A free trial with no credit card required, or a freemium option with limited features can be a good entry point for more skeptical users. But make sure to highlight the benefits to a paid upgrade.
  • Address objections - reduce friction by answering common questions like "Can I cancel anytime?" and "Do you offer refunds?". You can also reduce friction by showing social proof close to price display section (short testimonials or reviews).
  • You're allowed to use psychological tactics - scarcity and urgency are legitimate tools, but don't be deceptive. If you're showing a limited offer or a time-limited discount on your website, you can't just fake it and show it to everyone all the time.

3. Products section/page

Most of the tips I wrote for the pricing section/page, apply here as well. But if you're a small business focused on selling physical products, there are few more things to pay attention to:

  • High-quality visuals - multimedia content (images and videos) helps your customers visualize the product. So it's a good idea to use a high-quality media on your website. Make sure to showcase all of your product variations. But don't forget about the page loading speed (media files need to be optimized).
  • Short text - start with a clear and benefit-driven heading, followed by a one sentence subheading that reinforces the stated value. If needed add only a few bullet points. Everything else can be added under an expandable section titled " See more info".
  • Noticable CTAs - your main call-to-action button needs to stand out. You can use a sticky "Add to cart" button/icon to make it impossible to miss even while scrolling the page.
  • Use incentives - suggest complementary products, and offer product bundles if possible. If you can't offer free shipping as an incentive, you should show that upfront in the product's price.
  • Reviews - show at least some rating scores, if you don't have any reviews addressing common customer's concerns.
  • Display stock number - if there's a small number of products available (let's say under 10 items in stock), show that to trigger urgency.

4. 'Thank You' page

Your customer's journey doesn't just end when they finish the purchase. Them deciding to buy from you is a signal that they already know you and trust you. Why don't use that opportunity to build a long-term relationship with them?

It's a known fact that selling to existing customers is far easier than acquiring new customers. ' Thank you' page (triggered after purchase), is one way to nurture the relationship with your customers.

Here are ways to use the 'Thank you' page:

  • Outline next steps - don't leave your customers confused. Provide them with additional information about their purchase. This can be as simple as info about the delivery time (to set clear expectations), or any other relevant content. Show your contact info at the very end (or at least the link to FAQ section), for anyone needing assistance.
  • Engage them further - use the opportunity to deepen the connection with your customers. If you have a newsletter, or a free resource related to their purchase, share it. You can also ask them for honest feedback, by giving something in return (prize or a discount for the next purchase). This will give you useful data for improving your overall offer.
  • Show more products/services - offer them complementary products or services. Since they already bought from you, there's a high chance they would buy more. Use incentives (like discounts) for these additional offers, if the data shows low conversion rate of the 'Thank you' page.

5. Testemonials/Reviews

Reviews or testemonials are a form of social proof, that reduces your customer's anxiety and increases your credibility. Displaying them on specific spots throughout your website, can significantly increase the conversion rate.

Here's what you need to do:

  • Actively ask for reviews - don't be afraid to ask for reviews 1-2 weeks after purchase (when customers had the time to experience your product or service). That should be set automatically through your CRM software. Offering some incentive increases the likelihood they'll write a review. And make sure to ask people for their honest feedback, not just positive reviews.
  • Make it easy - provide them with the direct link that leads to the review form they need to fill out. Simplify the process by using well-known platforms for this (like Google Reviews). People are already familiar with using those platforms, plus it increases the credibility of those reviews.
  • Display them strategically - make sure to place them near call-to-action buttons (above the fold, and on pricing/product pages). If the reviews are lengthy or you have many of them - display the most interesting ones containing important keywords, because you can't fit it all in (especially on smaller screens). The full length reviews could be shown if the user clicks to do so (under collapsible section, pop-up, or taken to reviews page).
  • Respond to ratings and reviews - thank customers for their positive reviews, and adress the concerns expressed in negative ones. Don't be afraid to display your responses with the reviews - it shows you're oriented towards customer satisfaction.

6. Pop-ups

If you ask me personally - I don't like pop-ups, especially when they interupt me while I'm scrolling down the page. But the data shows pop-ups can be highly effective at conversion. That's because they bring your call-to-action into focus, forcing people to either take the action or close it.

When it comes to pop-ups, this is what you need to do:

  • Optimize the timing - don't just annoy people by triggering the pop-up on page load. Make sure to allow people to be on the page for at least 30 seconds. Or if it's a short page, when they scroll down the page past 40% of it's length. This indicates they didn't just land on your page by accident, so it's more likely they'll take the desired action instead of closing the pop-up. You can also use the exit-intent pop-ups triggered when a visitor tries to leave the page, as a last attempt to make them convert.
  • Customize based on the user behavior - show different content in your pop-ups for first time visitors, compared to the returning visitors. You could also show a different pop-up based on the exact page they're currently browsing. When you make the call-to-action as relevant as possible for that visitor, you increase the chance they'll take the desired action.
  • Make it compelling by design - keep it short, because the fewer the number of fields, the higher the conversion. Collect only the data you truly need. Clearly explain the value they get (especially in the CTA button). Use incentives like a lead magnet freebie, or a limited time offer to boost the conversion.
  • Don't overdo it - trigger one pop-up during s single website visit, regardless of the pages they visit. If you spam them with too many pop-ups, they'll just leave. The most relevant page to trigger it on is the one they land on first (especially if it's not your homepage).

7. FAQ section

In the world of AI search, FAQs are a good way to end up in the search results with short and direct answers to specific user inquiries. It also helps you convert people already interested in your product/service, that just need a bit of additional reassurance.

Here's what you should do with your FAQs:

  • Group the related questions - use clear headings for the grouped sections, with collapsible answers (to reduce scroll fatigue). Also, prioritize high-intent questions, because those signal someone is ready to take a specific action.
  • Match search intent - always use the language that your target audience uses and understands (avoid jargon). Give clear and actionable answers (up to few sentences). If there's more info on the subject, provide a link for those interested.
  • Use call-to-action buttons - add specific CTAs near relevant questions. You can also use links to guide people to the logical next step (like a specific product/service page).
  • Keep it engaging - if there's a lot of text in your FAQs, you might consider using images or videos to provide answers to your customers.
  • Provide a search option - if there's a lot of questions in your FAQs section, make sure to offer a search functionality. Such feature increses the likelihood people will find the answer they're looking for (making them one step closer to becoming your customer).
  • Update it regularly - your FAQ page should be updated from time to time, based on customer feedback, general search trends, and your website analytics.
