Conversion rate optimization for small business websites

If you downloaded my 'Website Foundations' checklist, you already fixed all of the technical aspects of your small business website (speed, accessibility, security and privacy).

If not, grab it now, and first go through the basics. Just follow the short weekly tutorials you'll get in your email inbox.

Now we need to look at all of the individual parts of your website, and optimize them for conversion. Because each of those small improvements contribute to the overall increase of your website's conversion rate.

We'll start with the parts of your website that most visitors see immediately after webpage loads (at the top of the page), then we'll discuss parts that only highly engaged visitors come across.

1. Navigation

Website navigation is usually the first thing we encounter, given the fact that it's on the very top of every webpage. Besides, we're all used to checking the navigation to see what's available on a website. Makes sense to optimize this section of your small business website first. Here's how:

  • Keep it simple - limit the menu items to the most important webpages. Otherwise you risk overwhelming the users of your small business website. Everything else can be placed somewhere else on the page (for example in the footer section).
  • Make it intuitive - use clear (self-explanatory) labels, and don't be misleading. First and last items in a list are usually most memorable to people, so use it to your advantage.
  • Check if the menu behaves as expected, especially on smaller screens. Keep in mind that the web browsing on mobile devices is ever increasing.

In most use cases I recommend using a sticky header section, since it makes the navigation easily accessible even when scrolling webpages with longer content.

2. Search

For any website with a lot of content, it's necessary to have a good search functionality. It doesn't matter if it's just blog posts, or a bunch of products on e-commerce store. And we know that a small business with limited marketing budget needs a decent content library for organic reach.

Now let's talk about the search feature best practices:

  • Place the search feature in usual location (in the header section), so users can find it instantly.
  • Allow users to filter the search results, especially if you have a lot of different categories of posts or products.
  • Provide real-time suggestions and popular search terms to guide users while they're typing. This reduces typos or dead ends.
  • Search results need to be visually appealing, with clear images, prices, and CTAs, not just plain links. Reduce the steps needed for them to take the desired action.
  • Make sure you handle the “no results” scenario by suggesting similar categories of products or content, and keep the users engaged.
  • Check the data on search queries - if people are often searching for something you don't offer, you can consider adding it to your offer.

3. Hero Section

If you're unfamiliar with the term, the hero section of a website refers to the area at the top of a webpage (above the fold). It's important because it's the first thing people see when the page loads, before they start scrolling and browsing the rest of the website.

Here's how to optimize the hero section of your small business website:

  • It should immediately communicate what you offer. Use a clear title about your core value proposition, and if needed a subtitle to elaborate on a key painpoint.
  • Place a single call-to-action button above the fold. Use action oriented text and a color that stands out from the background.
  • Avoid distractions by removing all unnecessary elements. The hero section should focus on one primary goal - for people to take the desired action (click the call-to-action button).
  • Everything else on a webpage can be loaded later, after the user scrolls (like lazy loading images when they enter the viewport). But the hero section must load as fast as possible on initial page load - so optimize the multimedia content.
  • If you're using a video, make it only few seconds long. But I always prefer using an image. And avoid text above images, because it can be difficult to read. I recommend using strategic whitespace between the hero image(s) and the rest (title, subtitle and CTA).
  • Make sure to reduce the content to bare essentials on smaller screens (to headline, call-to-action button, and a single supporting visual). The call-to-action button must be visible on all screen sizes without scrolling.

4. Footer

Footer section of a website is present on each webpage. Plus the users that scroll all the way down the page, are already engaged with your content, and there's a higher chance they'll convert to customers. This makes it a part of your website that you shouldn't overlook when optimizing for conversion.

Here are ways to improve the footer section of your small business website:

  • Make sure to put the most important call-to-action button in the footer also, just in case people missed it while scrolling and engaging with your content.
  • Put some contact information in the footer, and give an option to users to reach you directly. Remember that some people might have scrolled all the way down but haven't found any offer on your website that suits them. That doesn't mean they're not interested to work with you.
  • Include the links that could persuade those still indecisive about working with you - like FAQs page, some of your most popular blog posts (based on data), and the newsletter sign-up (or links to your social media).
  • Always include Privacy Policy, Terms of Service, and Copyright notices for compliance and transparency.

5. Blog

If you have a limited marketing budget, the best long-term return of investment is to create a decent content library. It doesn't matter if it's text, audio or video - but it should be a long form content that provides real value to your audience.

And that's why we have to talk about the blog section of your small business website. These webpages will help you get traffic not only from search engines, but also from social media (if you repurpose and share it).

This is the way to use your long-form content to it's fullest potential:

  • Before you decide what topic to do for every content piece, take the time to research and find related high-intent keywords. Those keywords will guide you towards creating content for users that are almost ready to take the desired action.
  • You should also align the content with your offers, using real case studies to showcase real value you provide to your clients. This can help you to insert call-to-action buttons in a way that feels more natural and less forced.
  • Use the intro of every content piece to hook people immediately with a clear, benefit-driven approach that addresses their pain points.
  • Format the content in a way that makes it easily scannable - by using short paragraphs, subheadings and bullet points.
  • Link to other related pages on your website, to keep visitors longer on your website and increase the chance they'll take the desired action (by clicking on the inserted call-to-action buttons).

Most importantly, try to be consistent with your content creation. You should make a schedule that works for you. It can be even once a month. It's better to take time and create a piece of content that's useful for your audience. You shouldn't feel forced to publish more frequently just for the sake of publishing.

6. Contact Page/Form

No matter what type of contact or scheduling form you have on your website, you should do the following:

  • Simplify the form - as few fields as possible. Ask only for essential information, because too many questions can frustrate people and reduce the completion rate. For anything non-essential, use optional fields (and mark them clearly as optional).
  • For spam protection use the honeypot field instead of CAPTCHA - it's less annoying to users. Honeypot fields is a hidden form field used to detect and block spam bots by baiting them into filling in data that invisible to human users.
  • Feature the privacy policy link, where you need to clearly state how you handle the data submitted through contact/scheduling form(s) on your small business website.
  • Always provide an alternative way of contacting you - by listing your email address, or at least provide your social media links. You never know what's people prefer to use as a direct contact channel.
  • Remove any distractions, or links that would lead them away from the contact form. The goal of this website section is for visitors to fill out and submit the form, nothing else.
  • If you're getting many repetitive inquiries, consider including the answer to those topics in your FAQs section.

7. 'About us' Page

The 'About us' page is a good opportunity to build trust between you and your potential clients. It's the place where you can share the story behind your small business. Here's what you need to pay attention to:

  • While this is a good spot to talk about your values and achievements, you need to frame that content around your client's needs and pain points. As always - write in a conversational tone, and avoid jargon that's difficult to understand.
  • Remember, it's still a content piece like any other. It needs to be engaging to people. So keep it short and straight to the point. Pick the most interesting facts about your business, you shouldn't write your whole history.
  • When you're using images or other media, make sure it's of good quality. Also, the media should be on brand - the colors and the mood must fit in the overall feel of your website. Don't just use something random.
  • It's a good idea to talk about exactly how you contribute to your client's success. Use few real examples of the work you've done, to highlight your unique approach and what sets you apart from your competitors.
  • If you're introducing your team, keep it lightweight and fun. It can be an opportunity for you to get to know them better, and allow them to contribute to your 'About us' page.

8. Landing Page

The conversion rate optimizations I've showed you so far, were about all of your webpages. It specially refers to your homepage - the page most users first land on.

But there's one type of webpages that are different to any other - those are landing pages.

Landing page is a standalone webpage, designed specifically for a marketing campaign. Unlike homepage or any other of your regular website pages - it's intended to remove all distractions and drive a single conversion goal (gathering leads or selling a product).

That's why you should remove any navigation leading to other pages of your small business website. Also, don't insert any outside link in a landing page content. Focus only on a single call-to-action (CTA).

Don't complicate it. All of the content on this page needs to persuade people to take the desired action and click on that CTA. Feel free to feature the same CTA throughout the landing page, wherever it makes sense.

Landing pages shouldn't be shown in the navigation on your regular webpages either. It can be confusing and frustrating for users who visit those pages, because there's no other option on those pages but to click on the CTA or close it. The only way to get to those pages is from an outside sources (you feature them in social posts, e-mails, etc.).

Lastly, don't forget to implement the same foundational steps for optimization of your landing pages (speed, accessibility, privacy and security).
